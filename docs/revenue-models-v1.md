> **One-line thesis:** Pure guiding is a lifestyle business at best and a weather-hostage at worst. The BnB basecamp is a seductive-but-premature capital trap. In Year 1 we should prove guiding + build a content moat; the right **force-multiplier in Year 2 is B2B whitelabel + corporate team-building** (not a property). A basecamp is a Year 3 decision, earned by data — not a launch bet.

## 1. Why pure guiding may not be enough

### Unit economics (estimate — all numbers below are my math; challenge them)

Base-case founder income if we run the obvious model:

| Input | Assumption | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ADR (price per person per day) | **NT$3,500** | Mid-range between Island Life NT$2,200 and JUST NT$4,800–6,400 ([competitive-scan §3](./competitive-scan-v1.md)) |
| Avg pax per trip | **5** | Regulation caps at 1 guide : 5 pax under 辦理溯溪活動應注意事項 ([regulatory §1](./regulatory-v1.md)) |
| Tradable days / year | **100** | ~6 good months (Apr–Oct) × ~4 weekend-days/week, minus weather cancellations, minus typhoons Jun–Oct |
| Trips / tradable day | **1** | Single day-trip window; unrealistic to double-run with 4 side-project founders |
| Gross revenue / trip | 3,500 × 5 = **NT$17,500** | |
| Annual gross revenue | 17,500 × 100 = **NT$1,750,000** | |
| Net margin after guide pay, gear, insurance, OTA take (15–25%), fuel, permits | **40%** | Generous — OTA commission alone is 15–25% ([regulatory §5.3](./regulatory-v1.md)) |
| **Annual net (pre-tax, pre-salary-to-founders)** | **NT$700,000** | |
| Split 4 ways | **NT$175,000/founder/year** | ≈ one month of a Taipei analyst salary |

**Sensitivity:**
- If we push to ADR NT$4,500 (premium positioning from [competitive-scan §6](./competitive-scan-v1.md)) and hit 6 pax average (hiring a second 嚮導): revenue 4,500 × 6 × 100 = NT$2.7M → net ~NT$1.08M → **NT$270k/founder**. Still below minimum-wage-equivalent for the hours.
- If we run **2 parallel trips/day** (needs 2 lead guides with 溯溪嚮導 cert, which we don't have): revenue doubles → **NT$540k/founder**. This is the realistic ceiling for pure guiding.
- Weather downside: a bad typhoon year eats 20–30 days. Year 1 as expats-only is more like 30 tradable days → **NT$210k gross / ~NT$84k net total**. Founder-scale: rounding error.

### The three structural problems

1. **Hour-capped.** Revenue scales with guide-hours on water. 4 founders × 100 days × 1 trip = hard ceiling. The only levers are price, pax/trip, and a second daily trip — all bounded.
2. **Weather-hostage.** Typhoon season is exactly our peak season. No structural hedge.
3. **Capacity on peak days is tiny.** A Saturday in August with 3 booking requests but only 1 lead-guide certified = we turn down 2 groups. This is the "10 expat trials Year 1" ceiling from [scope-roadmap §Q3](./scope-roadmap-v1.md).

**Implication:** At least one revenue stream must be *non-guide-hour-linear*. That's the real test for any idea in §2.

## 2. Revenue stream catalog

Scoring rubric for **fit 1–5**: 5 = obvious yes, do it Year 1; 3 = plausible add later; 1 = kill it. Fit weighs (a) guide-hour independence, (b) capital fit to NT$100k, (c) side-project compatibility, (d) leverages a specific founder edge.

| # | Stream | Capital | Ongoing time | Margin | Reg burden | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pure guiding (baseline) | Low | Med | Med (40%) | High | 3 |
| 2 | BnB / guesthouse basecamp | **High** | **High** | Low–Med | **High** | 2 |
| 3 | Gear rental (in-house) | Low | Low | High (70%) | Low | 4 |
| 4 | Training / certification courses | Low | Med | High | Med | 2 (Y3+) |
| 5 | Corporate team-building | Low | Low | **High (55%+)** | Same as guiding | **5** |
| 6 | Content / media (YT/IG) | Low | **High** | Variable | None | **4** |
| 7 | Community / membership | Low | Med | High | None | 2 |
| 8 | Affiliate / hotel referral | Low | Low | High (pure take-rate) | None | 3 |
| 9 | **B2B whitelabel** (guide for agencies) | Low | Low | Med (30%) | Low (agency holds 旅行業 risk) | **5** |
| 10 | Event hosting (races, clinics) | Med | High (spike) | Med | High (crowd permits) | 2 |
| 11 | Own-brand product line | Med–High | High | Med | Low | 1 |
| 12 | Insurance/compliance consulting | Low | Low | High | None | 2 |
| 13 | JP-inbound concierge (Apple) | Low | Low | High | Needs partner 甲種 agency | **4** |

### One-paragraph takes

**1. Pure guiding (3/5).** Table stakes. Generates brand, reviews, training reps, real customer data. But as §1 showed, it caps at ~NT$270k/founder/year even in an optimistic premium world. Run it — but don't lie to ourselves that it's the business.

**2. BnB basecamp (2/5).** Treated in depth in §3. Short version: too capital-concentrated and operationally heavy for a pre-launch team with no proven guiding track record. The case gets stronger in Year 3 if we're turning away business. Today it's a distraction.

**3. In-house gear rental (4/5).** Wetsuit/helmet/shoe/throw-bag rental to (a) self-guided tourists, (b) smaller operators who lack our gear pile, (c) Taipei weekend-warriors heading to Nanao. NT$500/set/day × 5 sets = NT$2,500/day, maybe 50 rental-days Year 2 = NT$125k at ~70% margin. Small money, but it's **pure leverage on gear we already own** and the ops burden is "bag handover in Nanao." Plug it on top of guiding. Low capital, zero new regulatory surface.

**4. Training courses (2/5, but kill for Year 1–2).** We can't teach what we haven't done. We'd need (a) 溯溪嚮導 cert (Q2 2026), (b) 2+ years operating experience, and (c) brand authority to charge. Interesting Year 3+ idea (per-seat margin huge, non-weather-dependent, indoor classroom de-risks typhoon days), but proposing it now is hubris.

**5. Corporate team-building (5/5).** **The most under-discussed lever we have.** Weekday utilization = a second tradable day per week that's currently zero. ADR 2–3× tourist rate (NT$6,000–10,000/pp is normal for corporate outdoor packages in TW). Margin 55%+ because the group is pre-committed, transport often paid separately, no OTA take. Regulatory surface identical to guiding — same 嚮導, same insurance. Sales cycle longer (relationship-based) but Apple has HR connections at Japanese trading houses, Dino has Taipei finance network. **This is the obvious Year 2 add.**

**6. Content / media (4/5).** Non-obvious but real. Duncan is native-English, based in US, with time. A YouTube channel — "adventure Taiwan for foreigners who never heard of it" — costs ~NT$0 to start and compounds. Revenue lags (18–24 months to meaningful sponsor dollars), but the **real value is top-of-funnel for guiding** — every video is a customer-acquisition asset with zero per-unit cost. Also solves the weather problem: rained-out trip → B-roll day. **Start it Q3 2026 alongside soft launch**, not Year 3.

**7. Community / membership (2/5).** Paid FB/LINE group for TW outdoor community is a nice idea but (a) the TAM is small (TW domestic outdoor community is Chinese-speaking and already has free FB groups), (b) we'd be competing with potential partners, and (c) monetization is thin (NT$500/member × 200 members = NT$100k, not worth the moderation time). Revisit only if a content audience forces it.

**8. Hotel affiliate (3/5).** Partner with 3–5 English-friendly hotels in Yilan/Taipei (Silks, W, boutique Yilan 民宿 chains); concierge refers guests, we pay 10–15% rev-share. **Pure upside** — no capital, no ongoing ops once contracts signed. Risk: low conversion (concierges push whoever tipped them last). Do it, but don't model aggressive revenue off it. Good Year 1 activity for Luke.

**9. B2B whitelabel (5/5).** **The other underappreciated lever.** Taiwan has dozens of small Chinese-language 旅行社 selling multi-day TW itineraries to foreign groups who need a ground-operator for the "adventure day." They already carry 旅行業責任保險 and handle sales. We just provide the guided experience at ~NT$2,500/pp (30% less than our retail but zero acquisition cost, zero OTA take, zero booking infrastructure load). Luke's local network is the wedge. **Fastest way to fill weekday slots in Year 2.**

**10. Events (2/5).** Sexy, capital-intensive, risky. A 溯溪賽 race requires crowd-permit, higher insurance, sponsorship sales, and a team that's never run an event. Maybe Year 4 as a brand moment. Not a business.

**11. Own-brand apparel/snacks/guidebook (1/5).** Classic founder-distraction. Product design, MOQ capital, inventory, e-commerce ops. The margin story is a lie once you account for unsold inventory. The guidebook specifically could be a lead magnet (free PDF for email) — but not a standalone revenue line. Kill as revenue, keep the PDF-as-marketing idea.

**12. Compliance consulting (2/5).** Cute, but we'd be selling "we figured out TW 溯溪 regs" to the 5 other boutique operators who mostly already know. TAM: ~NT$500k/year split among existing consultants. Skip.

**13. JP-inbound concierge (4/5).** Apple's edge, genuine whitespace per [competitive-scan §2.5](./competitive-scan-v1.md) (zero dedicated JP adventure operators, 2M+ JP visitors/year). Model: Apple builds relationship with VELTRA / Locotabi / HIS-adventure as the go-to JP adventure ground-ops. Bundle our Yilan trip + hotel + meal through a partner 甲種 agency. Margin thin on bundling (5–10%) but the **trip itself fills at premium rate**. Compounds with streams 5 and 9.

**Not scored but flagged:** end-of-season used-gear sales (one-time cash), paid speaking / podcast (Dino/Duncan long tail), "adventure photography" add-on at NT$1,500/trip (easy upsell, 90% margin).

## 3. Deep dive: BnB basecamp

The founder's favorite alt-idea. Here's the honest model.

### 3.1 Location candidates

| Location | Proximity to rivers | Foreign traffic | Property cost | Seasonality | Accessibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nanao (Yilan)** | 5 min to 鹿皮溪/金岳 trailheads | Low direct, high via Taipei (2hr) | **Low** — rural, old houses NT$5–15M purchase, rent NT$15–30k/mo | Jun–Oct typhoon-heavy | Taipei 2hr by car, 宜蘭 line train |
| **Hualien (Taroko adjacent)** | 20–40 min to Sanzhan, Jade Valley | **High** direct foreign traffic | Med — post-quake soft, NT$10–25M | Year-round but post-2024-quake access still staged ([regulatory §6.1](./regulatory-v1.md)) | Train from Taipei 2–3hr; airport |
| **Maolin (Pingtung)** | 15 min to 濁口 tributaries | Very low | Lowest — rural Rukai territory, NT$4–10M or rent cheap | Year-round warm | 1hr from Kaohsiung airport |
| **Ruisui (Hualien)** | 30 min to East Rift canyons | Med (hot spring tourism) | Med — average homestay NT$69–112/night per Booking.com [[Booking.com Ruisui homestays](https://www.booking.com/homestay/city/tw/jui-sui.html)] | Year-round, hot springs winter pull | Train access good, between Hualien city + Taitung |

**Rank for our business:** Nanao > Ruisui > Maolin > Hualien-Taroko. Nanao wins on guiding synergy; Ruisui wins on year-round occupancy; Hualien loses on post-quake uncertainty; Maolin requires 6–12mo of 魯凱族 community work before we could even break ground.

### 3.2 Regulatory path — the single biggest trap

Under **民宿管理辦法** (交通部觀光署; last amended 2019-10-09) [[民宿管理辦法 全國法規](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=K0110012)] [[觀光署 民宿登記說明](https://admin.taiwan.net.tw/BusinessInfo/Articles?a=259)]:

- **General zones:** max **5 rooms, total room floor area ≤150 m²**
- **Special zones** (原住民保留地, 休閒農業區, 觀光地區, **偏遠地區 + offshore**): max **15 rooms, ≤200 m²**
- Nanao qualifies as 原住民保留地 in parts + 偏遠地區 → **15-room ceiling applies**. Maolin same. Ruisui partially. Hualien city proper: 5-room cap.
- Must register 民宿登記證 with county government; requires building-use zoning that permits 住宿, fire inspection, public liability insurance (overlaps our existing regulatory surface).
- **Property must be owner-occupied / family-operated** in the legal definition — a pure investment rental operated by hired staff is *not* 合法民宿. Hard constraint for us.

**The Airbnb trap.** 88% of Taipei/NT Airbnb listings are illegal [[CNA 2022 illegal BnB exposé](https://www.cna.com.tw/project/20220930-illegal-bnb/)]. Any stay <30 days without 旅館 or 民宿 license = illegal 日租套房. **Fines: NT$100k–500k, recently raised to max NT$2M with consecutive penalties** [[北市觀傳局 稽查新聞稿](https://www.tpedoit.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=603755835E928BED&sms=72544237BBE4C5F6&s=F9F43C9BB9D990F6)]. "We'll just Airbnb it" is not a strategy for a business that also needs 嚮導 certs and 公共意外責任保險 carriers to take us seriously — one illegal-rental complaint and the whole operation's insurability is at risk.

**Translation:** BnB means real 民宿登記, real fire safety, real zoning, real on-site resident/operator. Not a weekend side-hustle.

### 3.3 Capital stack

Two realistic paths:

**Path A — Rent:** Nanao old house, NT$20k/mo rent × 12 = NT$240k/yr + 6mo key deposit (NT$120k). Renovation to 民宿 spec (fire safety, 3–5 rooms): **NT$800k–1.5M**. Furnishing: NT$300k. **Year-1 cash need: ~NT$1.5–2M.**

**Path B — Buy:** Nanao 2-story farmhouse NT$8–12M + renovation NT$1.5–2.5M = **NT$10–14M**. With 30% down + mortgage: equity ~NT$3–4M upfront, debt service NT$40–55k/mo.

Both paths are **10–40× our NT$100k Year 1 budget**. Requires outside capital — founder family, silent investor, or personally-guaranteed bank loan.

### 3.4 Revenue model (estimate)

Nanao 5-room 民宿, realistic inputs:
- **ADR:** NT$3,500/room/night (weekday–weekend blend, per [tw-bnb.com Nanao listings](https://tw-bnb.com/na.php))
- **Occupancy:** 35% Year 1, 50% stabilized (TW 民宿 avg 40–45%; Nanao seasonal skew: 70%+ weekends summer, <20% winter weekdays)
- **Rooms × days × occ × ADR:** 5 × 365 × 0.45 × 3,500 = **NT$2.87M gross lodging revenue at steady state**
- **Tour attach:** 30% of room-nights convert to a tour at NT$3,500/pp × 1.5 pax/room = NT$1,575/room-night × 5 × 365 × 0.45 × 0.30 = **NT$388k incremental tour revenue**
- **Operating costs:** property manager (NT$35k/mo = 420k/yr), cleaner (NT$200k/yr), utilities + supplies (NT$180k/yr), rent or mortgage (NT$240k–600k/yr), insurance + permits (NT$80k/yr), booking platform fees 15% = **NT$1.4–1.8M/yr**
- **Steady-state net:** ~NT$1.0–1.4M/yr lodging + full margin on tour attach → **NT$1.4–1.8M net combined, post stabilization**

**Break-even on Path A (rent):** Month 18–24 if occupancy tracks. Path B (buy): Month 36+ before mortgage is covered without founder labor.

### 3.5 Operating model problem

**None of the 4 founders can live in Nanao in Year 1.** That means:
- (a) Hire a manager (NT$35k/mo + housing) — wipes margin and you're trusting a stranger with your brand at launch, OR
- (b) Partner with an existing Nanao 民宿 owner — give up 30–50% of economics and lose control, OR
- (c) Wait until one of us can relocate (Duncan Year 2 relocates to TW but to where — Taipei or Nanao?).

Option (c) is the only one that preserves unit economics. And (c) is a Year 2–3 question, not a Year 1 one.

### 3.6 Why it could be transformative

- **Captive funnel.** Every guest is a pre-warmed tour lead. 30%+ attach rate realistic because they're already there with gear available.
- **Weather hedge.** Rainy day = beds still sell. Lodging revenue is uncorrelated with river conditions short-run.
- **Brand anchor.** "Come stay at our canyon basecamp" beats "meet at the 7-Eleven." Physical presence signals permanence to OTAs and agencies.
- **Community.** Nanao is small. A well-run foreign-friendly base becomes the *default* English-speaking adventure node — compounds.

### 3.7 Why it could sink us

- **Capital concentration at the riskiest stage.** Sinking NT$2M+ into a property before we've proven we can fill 30 guided trips/year is textbook "bet everything on the adjacent business before winning the core one."
- **Operational complexity ≠ complementary skills.** None of the 4 have run a hotel. Hospitality ops is a real discipline.
- **Dilutes focus.** Pre-launch, every hour on property due-diligence is an hour not on guide certs, website, sales, permits.
- **Illiquid exit.** If we pivot at Month 18, we can walk from a guiding LLC. We cannot walk from a mortgage or 5-year lease without massive losses.

**Verdict:** NOT a Year 1 or Year 2 play. Structured as a Year 3 decision gate (§5).

### 3.8 Reference models (what "good" looks like elsewhere)

- **NZ guided river ops** mostly cluster at put-ins and partner with nearby lodges rather than owning lodging. [Hidden Valleys Rafting](https://www.hiddenvalleysrafting.co.nz/) "can arrange" accommodation. [Rafting New Zealand](https://raftingnewzealand.com/) in Turangi same. Even 25+yr operators haven't vertically integrated into beds — suggesting the hybrid is *harder than it looks even in mature markets*.
- **Colorado guide-ranches** (Vista Verde, C Lazy U, 4UR Ranch) — lodging-first with guiding as upsell, not guiding-first with lodging bolt-on. Different primitive.
- **TW precedent:** Taiwan Adventures, Island Life, Hualien Outdoors — **none** own lodging. Every successful English-first TW adventure op is asset-light. That's a signal.

**Read:** The cases where lodging + guiding works are lodging-led businesses that added guiding. There is almost no case of a small guiding startup acquiring lodging as Phase 2 and succeeding. Respect that base rate.

## 4. Alternative business structures

### 4.1 Social enterprise / 社會創新組織登錄

Taiwan has a voluntary **社會創新組織登錄** register run by 經濟部 (si.taiwan.gov.tw). It's a *registry*, not a separate legal entity. Benefits: access to gov procurement, incubator programs, grants, soft PR. **There is no direct corporate-tax exemption** tied to SE registration in current TW tax law [[PwC Taiwan Tax Update Dec 2025](https://www.pwc.tw/en/publications/taiwan-tax-updates/assets/taiwan-tax-update-202512.pdf)].

Where it matters for us: if we hire Atayal community members in Nanao as junior guides and frame the business around indigenous-youth employment + river-access conservation, we unlock:
- Ministry of Culture / Council of Indigenous Peoples project grants (typical NT$200k–1M)
- 林業署 / Forestry Agency co-management pilots
- Warmer reception on 林班地 / 原住民保留地 access (currently an open question per [regulatory §8](./regulatory-v1.md))

**Verdict:** Register in Year 2 once we have hiring to point to. Not a Year 1 priority. Real value is *relationship leverage in Nanao*, not tax.

### 4.2 Cooperative / 合作社

Under 合作社法: members share ownership; revenue distributed per participation. Could work if we want to formally include Nanao local guides as worker-owners rather than contractors.

- **Pro:** reduces capital burden (members contribute), aligns incentives with local community (strong for indigenous-land access), unique story for content channel.
- **Con:** governance complexity with 7+ members, slow decisions, unfamiliar structure for foreign investors (problematic for Duncan's FIA path per [regulatory §3.2](./regulatory-v1.md)).

**Verdict:** Interesting only if we pivot hard toward community-owned. Conflicts with current 70/30 TW/US 有限公司 plan. **Skip for now**, revisit if Nanao community relationship gets deep.

### 4.3 Franchise / license model

Year 3+: once we have a proven ops playbook, license brand + SOP + booking system to a Hualien or Pingtung operator for royalty. Economics: NT$200–500k upfront + 5–8% of revenue.

**Verdict:** Real option but franchise history says don't franchise until the core unit is provably replicable — 3+ years of consistent unit economics and documented ops. Park this.

### 4.4 Acquisition

The asymmetric option nobody usually considers. If a boutique operator (e.g., a fading Chinese-language Nanao op, a small 民宿 with existing 溯溪 license, a struggling post-quake Hualien shop) is willing to sell at the right multiple — it could shortcut years of relationship-building and permit-acquisition.

- **Cost range (estimate):** small TW outdoor shop with 2–4 years of trading, some gear, some reviews, existing county filings: ~NT$1.5–4M (2–3× SDE).
- **Where the alpha is:** permits and community relationships, not gear.
- **Specific targets to watch:** any Chinese-language Nanao ops if founders retire; struggling post-quake Hualien shops; a Maolin operator tired of running the 魯凱 relationship.

**Verdict:** **Keep on radar, actively.** Luke should put out feelers in Q3 2026 regardless — even just to learn pricing. Costs nothing to ask.

## 5. Sequencing recommendation — 3-year phased roadmap

### Year 1 (Apr 2026 – Apr 2027): Prove guiding + seed content

**Revenue streams active:**
- **Pure guiding** (baseline — day-trip SKU, §2.1)
- **Content channel** (Duncan YouTube/IG, start Q3 2026 alongside soft launch, §2.6) — zero revenue Year 1, building audience

**Target:** 10+ paid foreign-tourist trips per [scope-roadmap Q4](./scope-roadmap-v1.md), NT$300–500k revenue, founder compensation zero. **Goal is proof, not profit.**

**Explicitly NOT in Year 1:** BnB, team-building sales, training courses, events, product line. If anyone suggests these, point them to this doc.

### Year 2 (Apr 2027 – Apr 2028): Fill weekdays, compound content

**Add (priority order):**
1. **B2B whitelabel** (§2.9) — Luke sells to 3–5 Chinese-language 旅行社 needing adventure ground-ops. Target 30 whitelabel days at NT$2,500/pp.
2. **Corporate team-building** (§2.5) — Apple + Dino open 5 corporate accounts (Japanese trading houses, US-HQ MNCs with TW offices, finance teams). Target 10 corporate days at NT$7,000/pp.
3. **Gear rental** (§2.3) — small bolt-on, NT$100k side revenue.
4. **JP-inbound concierge** (§2.13) — Apple builds VELTRA / Locotabi relationship, bundles with our Yilan SKU.
5. **Hotel affiliate** (§2.8) — Luke signs 3 Yilan/Taipei hotels.
6. **Content monetization begins** — if channel crosses 10k subs, first sponsor/affiliate revenue.
7. **Register 社會創新組織** if we've hired an Atayal junior guide (§4.1).

**Target:** NT$2–3M total revenue, ~NT$400–500k/founder. **First year someone could go part-time.**

### Year 3 (Apr 2028 – Apr 2029): Physical expansion *if* metrics hit

**Gate criteria to trigger basecamp investment (ALL must be true):**
- Year 2 revenue ≥ NT$2.5M
- ≥ 80 tradable days fully booked (turning away ≥20 groups)
- At least one founder relocated to or willing to relocate to the basecamp
- External capital secured (family, silent investor, or bank loan) without risking founder personal solvency
- Duncan back in TW and free to commit

**If all green → BnB basecamp** (§3) via rental path in Nanao, 5-room 民宿 registration, tour attach model.
**If not green → delay or kill.** Alternative Year 3 moves: training courses (§2.4), franchise exploration (§4.3), or opportunistic acquisition (§4.4).

## 6. Open questions

Things we need to resolve before committing real money to anything beyond guiding:

1. **Family capital.** Do any of the 4 founders have access to NT$2M+ low-cost family capital for a Year 3 basecamp? If zero → BnB path dies regardless of economics.
2. **Duncan's TW relocation.** Year 2 move to *where* — Taipei (default) or Yilan/Nanao (basecamp-enabling)? Changes the whole §5 sequence.
3. **Corporate pipeline reality check.** Can Apple + Dino book 3 discovery calls with potential corporate clients in Q4 2026 (before launch) to validate team-building demand? If "HR doesn't call back," stream §2.5 drops from 5/5 to 3/5.
4. **B2B partner availability.** Can Luke identify 3 Chinese-language 旅行社 willing to sign a whitelabel SOW by Q2 2027? Name names. If vague, §2.9 is a hypothesis, not a plan.
5. **Content commitment.** Is Duncan willing to publish 1 video/week for 18 months before meaningful revenue? If not, §2.6 is a fantasy — kill now rather than dribble half-effort.
6. **Japanese-market economics.** What does a VELTRA bundle actually pay per JP head? Apple should get one real quote from a VELTRA regional manager before we model §2.13 as 4/5.
7. **Insurance coverage scope.** Does our 公共意外責任保險 carrier cover team-building (higher-risk headcount), whitelabel trips (where we're not the seller), and gear rental (third-party use)? One call to the broker.
8. **民宿 zoning at candidate Nanao properties.** Before any Year 3 decision, 3–5 specific property addresses checked for 住宿 zoning + 偏遠地區 designation. One-afternoon task with Yilan 觀光處 — worth doing in Year 2 just to have data.
9. **Acquisition targets.** Any Nanao/Hualien/Maolin operators quietly for sale? Luke should ask 3 people in Q3 2026. Free intel.
10. **Founder employment structure.** If any founder goes full-time Year 2, does the 有限公司 pay a salary (triggering 勞健保 + payroll) or dividend-only? Accountant question — materially affects team-building and whitelabel economics.

## Top recommendation

**Do pure guiding + content in Year 1. Add B2B whitelabel and corporate team-building in Year 2 — that's the force multiplier, not a property. Treat the BnB basecamp as a Year 3 decision gated on Year 2 performance, and spend exactly zero founder-hours on property scouting until then. The asymmetric side bet to keep alive is an opportunistic acquisition of an existing small operator; Luke should put out quiet feelers by Q3 2026.**

The founder concern that "pure guiding isn't sustainable" is correct. The answer is not to add beds — it's to add **weekday utilization (corporate + B2B) and zero-marginal-cost funnels (content)**. Those two together break the guide-hour ceiling without breaking the balance sheet.
